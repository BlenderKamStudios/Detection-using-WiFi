What am I looking at?

Four ESP32 boards talk over Wi-Fi: TX sends 100 small packets per second, and three receivers (RX1, RX2, RX3) placed around the room pick them up. That makes three links across the room. A person near a link changes how the Wi-Fi signal bounces, and the program measures that change on each link.

  • Glowing ovals between TX and each receiver: the detection area of each link. An oval lights up red when that link sees movement.
  • Moving dots on the lines: the Wi-Fi packets, always from TX (sender) to a receiver, 100 per second each. They scatter when something moves.
  • Glowing ball: appears when someone is detected, placed near the link(s) that see the most movement. The arrow shows which way the movement goes. It swells with each breath when breathing is found.
  • Mountain surface behind: the live Wi-Fi pattern, one line per frequency, newest at the front. Spikes = something moving.

What it cannot do

The position is coarse: three links tell roughly which part of the room the movement is in, not an exact spot, and not how many people there are. A still person can be detected by breathing, but not located. Board positions must be entered in layout.json for the map to match the room.

How to test

  1. Walk across the room through the links: the top should turn red, "SOMEONE IS MOVING", and the arrow should point the way you walk.
  2. Sit still with your chest close to one of the lines for about 20-30 s: breathing should be found (green).
  3. Leave: after a minute it returns to blue, "NOBODY DETECTED".
